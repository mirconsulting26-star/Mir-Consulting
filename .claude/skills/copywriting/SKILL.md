# Copywriting

Creates persuasive, benefit-driven copy for landing pages, ads, emails, and product pages.

## STEP 1: UNDERSTAND THE BRIEF

Parse $ARGUMENTS for:
- Format: landing page, hero section, ad copy, product page, pricing page, CTA button
- Product or service: what is being sold
- Target audience: who is reading this, what do they care about?
- Primary competitor or alternative: what are they currently doing instead?
- Desired action: what should the reader do?

## STEP 2: IDENTIFY THE CORE MESSAGE

Before writing, answer these three questions:
1. **What is the specific outcome the customer gets?** (not a feature — a result)
2. **What is the deepest pain this solves?** (not a surface inconvenience — the real cost of the problem)
3. **Why should they believe you?** (proof, specificity, credibility)

Every piece of copy flows from these answers.

## STEP 3: WRITE THE COPY

### For landing page hero sections:
```
Headline: The outcome in ≤10 words (be specific, not clever)
Subheadline: How you deliver it + who it's for (1-2 sentences)
Primary CTA: Action-oriented, benefit-focused button text
Social proof: 3-5 logos or a stat that builds credibility
```

### For ad copy (Facebook/Google):
- **Hook (first line)**: Must stop the scroll. Speak to the pain or trigger curiosity.
- **Body**: Specific benefit + brief credibility signal
- **CTA**: What to do next

### For product pages:
- Lead with the benefit, not the feature
- Every feature has a "which means you can..." follow-through
- Use specifics: "saves 3 hours per week" beats "saves time"

## STEP 4: APPLY THE SPECIFICITY TEST

For every claim, ask: "Can I make this more specific?"
- "Saves time" → "Saves 4.5 hours per week"
- "Trusted by businesses" → "Trusted by 3,200 growth teams"
- "Easy to use" → "Set up in 5 minutes, no code required"

More specific = more believable.

## STEP 5: WRITE THREE VARIANTS

Produce three versions:
1. **Direct**: Leads with the outcome, minimal framing
2. **Problem-first**: Opens with the pain, then introduces the solution
3. **Social proof-first**: Opens with a customer result, then explains how

## LINKEDIN CONTENT

LinkedIn has distinct content mechanics that differ from ad copy or landing pages. Apply this section when writing LinkedIn posts, carousels, or thought leadership content.

### Hook Engineering

Every LinkedIn post requires a hook in the first line that earns the "...see more" click. Write three variants per post:

```
Hook 1 (Curiosity Gap):    Implies a surprising outcome without revealing it.
                           "I almost turned down the job that changed my career."

Hook 2 (Bold Claim):       States a provocative position directly.
                           "Your LinkedIn headline is why you're not getting recruiter messages."

Hook 3 (Specific Story):   Opens mid-scene with a concrete detail.
                           "Tuesday, 9 PM. I'm about to hit send on my resignation email."
```

Choose the variant that withholds the key insight — the goal is the click to expand, not to summarize the post in the opener.

### Post Type Structures

**Story Post**: Specific moment → tension or setback → resolution → transferable insight. Never vague. Never "I learned so much from this." Name the exact moment, the exact decision, the exact outcome.

**Expertise Post**: State what most people get wrong about the topic → introduce the correct mental model → prove it with a concrete example or data point. Structure: "Most people think X. Here's why that's wrong, and what actually works."

**Opinion Post**: Lead with the take → acknowledge the strongest counterargument → defend the position with evidence → invite the reply. Never soften the opening take to avoid controversy — neutral gets ignored.

### Carousel Architecture

- **Lead slide**: Must function as a standalone post. If the reader never swipes, the lead slide alone should deliver value and create the pull to swipe.
- **Interior slides**: One idea per slide. One visual or data point. Maximum 15 words of body copy. Each slide builds toward the reveal.
- **Reveal slide** (second to last): The payoff — the insight the whole carousel built toward.
- **Final slide**: Specific CTA tied to the carousel topic + follow prompt + "save for later" if reference-worthy.

Upload carousels as native PDF documents, not images — LinkedIn gives native documents significantly more reach.

**Formatting rules for all LinkedIn posts**
- One idea per paragraph, maximum 2-3 lines
- No external links in post body — LinkedIn suppresses them; put links in the first comment
- 3-5 specific hashtags maximum (specific > generic: `#b2bsales` over `#business`)
- Tag people only when genuinely relevant — tag spam kills reach

## ANTI-PATTERNS

- Features instead of benefits ("Our dashboard has 45 metrics" vs "See exactly which ads drive revenue")
- Vague claims that sound like every other product ("best-in-class", "game-changing", "innovative")
- Clever wordplay that obscures meaning — clarity beats wit
- Writing for everyone — great copy speaks directly to one person
- "We" language — too much focus on the company, not the customer
