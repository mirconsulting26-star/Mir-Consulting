# Cold Email Writer

Writes personalized, high-converting cold outreach emails that get replies. Based on B2B outreach best practices.

## STEP 1: GATHER CONTEXT

Parse $ARGUMENTS for:
- Target prospect (company, role, name)
- Your product or service
- The specific pain point or value proposition
- Desired call-to-action (book a call, reply to confirm interest, try a free tool)

If any context is missing, ask for it before writing.

## STEP 2: RESEARCH THE PROSPECT — SIGNAL-BASED OPENING

Before writing, identify the buying signal that triggered this outreach. Signals ranked by intent strength:

- **Active buying signals** (highest priority): G2/review site activity, pricing page views, competitor comparison searches, RFP announcements, technology evaluation job postings
- **Organizational change signals**: New VP/Director hire in your buyer's function, funding event (Series B+ with stated growth goals), hiring surge in the department your product serves, M&A activity
- **Technographic/behavioral signals**: Technology stack change, conference attendance on adjacent topics, content engagement (whitepapers, webinars), competitor contract renewal timing

Reference the specific signal in the opening line — this is what separates 12-25% reply rates from 1-3%. If no clear signal exists, use recent company news, a published article, or a relevant job posting as a secondary fallback.

**Speed matters**: The half-life of a buying signal is short. Outreach within 24 hours of a signal converts at 4-8x untriggered cold outreach. After 72 hours, a competitor has likely already had the conversation.

## STEP 3: WRITE THE EMAIL

Follow the AIDA framework adapted for cold email:

**Subject line**: Specific, intriguing, < 50 characters. No clickbait.
- Good: "Re: your new checkout flow"
- Bad: "Boost your revenue by 300%!!!"

**Opening (1 sentence)**: Reference the personalized detail from Step 2. This is your only shot at relevance.

**Pain/Problem (1-2 sentences)**: Describe a problem you believe they have. Be specific to their role and industry.

**Value proposition (1-2 sentences)**: How you solve that specific problem. No feature lists. One concrete outcome.

**Social proof (1 sentence, optional)**: A result from a similar company. Specific numbers beat vague claims.

**CTA (1 sentence)**: A single, low-friction ask. "Worth a 15-minute call?" beats "Please schedule a demo at your convenience."

**Total length**: 100-150 words. Respect their time.

## STEP 4: WRITE THREE VARIANTS

Produce three versions of the email with:
1. Different subject lines (question, statement, name-drop)
2. Different pain angles (if multiple apply)
3. Different CTAs (soft, medium, direct)

## REPLY RATE BENCHMARKS

Use these to calibrate expectations and measure sequence effectiveness:

| Outreach Type | Reply Rate |
|---|---|
| Generic, untargeted | 1-3% |
| Role/industry personalized | 5-8% |
| Signal-based with account research | 12-25% |
| Warm introduction or referral | 30-50% |

If you're below the signal-based benchmark, the opening line is the most likely weak point — it's not yet referencing a real reason to reach out right now.

## STEP 5: ANTI-PATTERNS TO AVOID

- Opening with "I hope this email finds you well"
- Talking about your company history before the prospect's problem
- Multiple CTAs (pick one)
- Attachments on first contact
- Paragraphs over 3 lines
- "Just following up" — a follow-up email needs a new reason to reach out
